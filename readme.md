1. Title: Analysis of Fast-Varying Instantaneous Frequency Signals: Insights from Window Selection and Synchrosqueezing Transform
2. Abstract: This paper constructs an analytical framework for signals containing multiple components with fast-varying instantaneous frequency oscillations. To describe such signals, the adaptive harmonic model is extended to include the rapid time-varying instantaneous frequency components. Additionally, Renyi entropy is employed to measure the time-frequency representation provided by the Synchrophasor Squeezing Transform (SST), thereby determining the optimal window width. For multicomponent signals with coupling artifacts, an adaptive optimal window method that segments the signal into multiple segments is proposed. The examples provided in the paper include a synthetic signal and its application in Atomic Time-Dependent Dipolar Moment Studies Driven by a Bichromatic Infrared Laser Field.
3. Introduction: Oscillations not only reflect the inherent characteristics of a system but also reveal interactions between systems. As a window into the system, studying the oscillatory behavior of signals is a fundamental topic. While Fourier transforms are widely used across various fields, they are not suitable for analyzing non-stationary dynamic processes. Consequently, time-frequency analysis techniques have emerged, offering the time-frequency representation (TFR) of signals. If constructed properly, TFR can be used to interpret the dynamic characteristics of the system from its time-frequency representation.
Over the past 70 years, various time-frequency analysis methods have been developed, primarily including linear transformations, quadratic transformations, and redistribution methods (RM). However, these methods each have their own limitations in capturing dynamic processes: linear transformations (such as Short-Time Fourier Transform, STFT) are limited by the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle; quadratic transformations (such as Wiener-Khinchin distribution) suffer from severe mode mixing artifacts; traditional redistribution methods can "enhance" the results of linear or quadratic transformations but lack an inverse transformation.
In recent years, the introduction of Synchronous Squeezed Transient Analysis (SST) has somewhat alleviated these issues. It is based on a selected linear transformation (such as Short-Time Fourier Transform), serving as a special case of redistribution methods. According to the redistribution rule that only considers the phase information of the linear transformation, it exhibits an "enhanced" oscillatory characteristic. Simultaneously, Synchronous SST retains the causality of signals, thus possessing the ability to reverse transformations and providing adaptive denoising solutions and component extraction techniques for multi-component signal processing.
Although Synchronous SST has been applied in various fields such as medicine, geography, physics, it often faces two practical issues in its application: first, theoretically, the time-frequency representation of Synchronous SST does not mainly rely on the selected window size, but the window size significantly affects the details of the time-frequency representation. How to select the optimal window according to specific criteria becomes key; second, when the oscillatory signal has a "fast time-varying" instantaneous frequency (IF), selecting the window width becomes complex. These problems stem from the Short-Time Fourier Transform or other linear transformations, and similar limitations exist in the second-order redistribution rule (second-order SST) and traditional redistribution techniques — improper window selection can lead to suboptimal time-frequency representation effects.
Given this, this paper explores the impact of window width on synchronous compression transform (SCT) under conditions of rapid time-varying instantaneous frequency components. The study shows that reducing the window width can effectively enhance the performance of SCT, second-order synchronous compression transform, and redistribution methods. In line with recent literature demands for short windows, a method based on Renyi entropy to determine the optimal window width is proposed (Renyi entropy can measure the diffusion degree of time-frequency representation). Besides theoretical derivations, two examples are also validated: synthetic data and simulated atomic time-varying dipole moment driven by a bichromatic infrared laser field.

Adaptive Harmonic Model: This model is used to describe the time-varying oscillatory dynamics in signals, especially for rapidly time-varying instantaneous frequency components. It defines specific parameters (such as small positive numbers ε, constants c₁, c₂, c₃, d, etc.), constructing a function set Qₑ,d^c₁,c₂,c₃. The signal x(t) in the set contains multiple inherent mode types (IMT) functions, each IMT function consisting of amplitude modulation (AM) aₖ(t) and instantaneous frequency (IF) φₖ'(t), where the instantaneous frequency interval between each IMT function satisfies certain conditions. Additionally, the dynamic existence of IMT functions (i.e., only existing within a specific time interval Iₖ) is considered.

Synchronous Compression Transform (SCT): The SCT in this paper adopts short-time Fourier transform (STFT)-based SCT, choosing the Gaussian function as the window function gₐ(t). Firstly, the short-time Fourier transform Vₓgₐ(u,η) of the signal x(t) about the window gₐ(t) is calculated; then, a synchronous compression transform S₉ₐ,ₓα,γ(u,ξ) is defined with resolution α and threshold γ. Through the redistribution rule ωₓ^γ(u,η), the coefficients at (u,η) in the short-time Fourier transform are reallocated to (u,ξ), achieving "sharpening" of the time-frequency representation. This redistribution process is nonlinear.
Application of Small Window in Synchronous Compression Transform: Theoretical proofs indicate that when the window width is sufficiently small and the instantaneous frequency intervals of various oscillation components are sufficiently large, synchronous compression transform can provide clear time-frequency representations. By defining a universal constant, we derive the error range of short-time Fourier transform and its derivatives with respect to the approximated signal, indicating that small windows can control the errors brought by rapid time-varying instantaneous frequencies while enhancing temporal resolution, aiding in extracting dynamic information from IMT functions (such as the moments of appearance and disappearance), and also approximate the signal's complex form through the inverse transformation formula.

Window selection based on Rayleigh entropy: Rayleigh entropy is used to measure the concentration of time-frequency representations, with higher entropy values indicating more dispersed time-frequency distributions. The Rayleigh entropy Rₐ for α>0 is defined, and the optimal window width σ is determined by finding the minimum value of Rayleigh entropy (i.e., σ=argminₐ>0 Rₐ). It is recommended in the paper that α>2, but in practice, α=2 is chosen. Although there are slight differences in results for different α values (α>0), they are acceptable.

Adaptive Optimal Window Method: To address the issue of global optimal windows failing to capture local details, this method divides the signal into multiple overlapping or non-overlapping segments. For each segment, an optimal window is sought, and then the results from all segments are combined to obtain the time-frequency representation of the entire signal, thereby enhancing the quality of the time-frequency representation.

Conclusion This paper investigates the adaptive harmonic model containing rapid time-varying instantaneous frequency components through simultaneous compression transformation, theoretically proving that selecting a smaller window width is more advantageous for capturing rapid time-varying instantaneous frequency components. To obtain the optimal window width, a method minimizing the time-frequency representation Renyi entropy is proposed. The performance of this method is verified through synthetic signals and physical observable measurements (atomic hydrogen electron dipole moment) examples. Additionally, it has been confirmed that the adaptive optimal window concept effectively enhances the quality of time-frequency representations.
